# Flatline Pest Technology — Content Strategy

## Content principles

### Lead with the customer's problem

Use recognizable concerns such as termite damage, roaches in a kitchen, rodents around a business, or lawn damage before discussing treatment technology.

### Make the next step concrete

Every important page should explain the same simple path: contact Flatline, arrange an inspection, then receive a tailored treatment plan and quotation. State clearly that inspections are free in Trinidad, while Tobago and other regional visits carry a travel-cost inspection fee.

### Earn trust with specifics

Prefer verifiable details—training frequency, insurance coverage, response process, follow-up, service area, and qualified warranty language—over generic claims such as “the best.”

### Preserve honest uncertainty

Treatment, timing, price, and warranty depend on the inspection. Explain variables clearly instead of using vague fine print.

### Design for urgent mobile use

Use short headings, direct answers, tap-to-call and WhatsApp actions, and forms that can be completed one-handed.

## Homepage narrative

1. Name the outcome and location.
2. Offer an inspection through WhatsApp, phone, or form and explain the location-based fee policy.
3. Show major pest problems and property types.
4. Explain why Flatline is a credible choice.
5. Explain the three-step service process.
6. Feature termite control and pre-construction protection.
7. Add approved reviews and proof.
8. Clarify coverage across Trinidad and Tobago.
9. Answer common objections and questions.
10. Repeat the inspection CTA.

## Proof placeholders for the first build

Use visibly labeled development placeholders rather than fabricated content:

- Three testimonial cards labeled “Customer review coming soon.”
- A treatment-results gallery labeled “Project photographs coming soon.”
- A team or technician image area labeled “Team photograph coming soon.”
- A training-proof area that can later display the supplied certificate after publication permission is confirmed.

Placeholders must not include invented names, ratings, quotations, project outcomes, or stock photography presented as Flatline's work. Development placeholders should be easy to remove before launch.

## Quote form strategy

Keep the first release short enough to complete in approximately one minute.

1. What problem are you experiencing?
2. What type of property needs service?
3. Where is the property located?
4. When would you prefer an inspection?
5. How should Flatline contact you?
6. Optional: add details or photographs.

Do not generate an automated price before enough real quotation data exists. The useful first automation is a structured lead summary sent to the business and a clear confirmation sent to the customer.

## Voice

Clear, calm, responsive, and capable. Avoid alarmist language, excessive technical terminology, and unsupported superlatives.

Use “pest control” when describing the category and plain pest names when helping people recognize their problem. Explain specialist terms at first use.
